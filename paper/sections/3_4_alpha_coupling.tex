\subsection{Consistent \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{α}-Coupling Across the PBD Pipeline}
\label{sec:alpha_coupling}

The cumulative damage formulation (Sec.~\ref{sec:pf_cumulative}) produces a
per-particle aliveness scalar $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$ via the sigmoid mapping
$\alpha_i = \sigma(s(\tau - d_i))$, where $d_i$ is the accumulated edge-damage
aggregate and $(s, \tau)$ are the sharpness and threshold parameters. We refer
to particles with $\alpha_i \in (\alpha_{\text{dead}}, \alpha_{\text{alive}})$,
for thresholds $\alpha_{\text{dead}}{=}0.01$ and
$\alpha_{\text{alive}}{=}0.5$, as residing in the \emph{transition phase} of
P(F) --- physically representing tissue that is partially detached but not yet
fully aspirated.

A standard PBD substep applies five operations to particle $i$:
(i)~prediction $\hat{p}_i \leftarrow x_i + v_i \, dt$ with damped velocity
$v_i \leftarrow \beta\,(v_i + g\,dt)$,
(ii)~collision projection,
(iii)~shape-matching cluster fit producing per-cluster goal positions $g_i^{(c)}$,
(iv)~shape correction application $x_i \leftarrow \hat{p}_i + k_i\,\sum_c (g_i^{(c)} - \hat{p}_i)$,
(v)~velocity update $v_i \leftarrow (x_i - x_i^{\text{old}})/dt$.

Each operation \emph{can} read $\alpha_i$ as a soft weight; we observe that
\emph{consistent} $\alpha$-coupling --- applying $\alpha_i$ to all kinematic
operations on particle $i$, not just shape-matching --- is essential for
physical realism during the transition phase.

\paragraph{Failure mode of partial coupling.}
Baseline differentiable PBD soft-removal formulations (e.g.,
DiSECt~\cite{diSECt2021}, Liang~\cite{liang2023}) couple $\alpha$ into
the shape-matching cluster fit (step iii) and the shape-correction stiffness
(step iv), but use a binary threshold $\alpha_i > \epsilon$ in the prediction
step (i) to gate momentum integration. This produces a kinematic-restoration
imbalance: partially-damaged particles ($\alpha_i = 0.3$) receive full
gravity integration and full knife contact impulse, yet experience only
$\alpha_i {=} 30\%$ of the cluster restoration force. Momentum accumulates
each substep, and we observe particles ejecting from the simulation domain
at velocities exceeding $5\,$m/s, producing implausible artifacts we term
\emph{zombie} particles (Fig.~\ref{fig:zombie}, baseline).
Across $10$-frame synthetic cube-cut sequences with $N{=}21745$ particles,
$305$ particles ($1.4\%$) are ejected beyond the cube boundary in the baseline.

\paragraph{Consistent coupling.}
We propose modifying the prediction step to apply $\alpha$-weighted damping:
\begin{equation}
v_i \leftarrow (\beta \cdot \alpha_i) \, (v_i + g\,dt)
\label{eq:alpha_predict}
\end{equation}
Healthy particles ($\alpha_i {\approx} 1$) experience the standard damping
coefficient $\beta$, while transition-phase particles experience a
proportionally amplified bleed-off of accumulated momentum. The hard
threshold $\alpha_i < \alpha_{\text{dead}}$ remains as a numerical
short-circuit for fully-failed particles. Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha_predict}
introduces no new parameters and preserves the gradient flow $\partial v_i /
\partial \tau$, $\partial v_i / \partial s$ through the sigmoid in $\alpha_i$,
sustaining sys-ID compatibility.

This single modification eliminates zombie ejection ($305 \to 0$ flyers) and
reduces maximum particle displacement by $13.8\times$ ($415\,$mm $\to 30\,$mm)
on the same sequence. More notably, it improves system identification
accuracy: inside-inclusion stiffness recovery error drops from $2.0\%$ to
$0.75\%$ on synthetic experiments (Sec.~\ref{sec:results},
Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), because the cleaner observation eliminates
spurious gradient contributions from ejected particles.

\paragraph{Persistence enforcement.}
The cumulative damage update is monotone by construction (softplus-smoothed
maximum). To enforce the corresponding $\alpha_i$ monotonicity strictly,
preventing numerical regression during transient overshoots, we apply:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i^{(t+1)} = \min\!\left(\sigma(s(\tau - d_i^{(t+1)})), \, \alpha_i^{(t)}\right)
\label{eq:alpha_persistence}
\end{equation}
matching the binary persistence semantic of mesh-detachment cutting
algorithms (e.g., CUSA aspiration~\cite{cppsim}) while preserving
differentiability via the smooth minimum.

\paragraph{Summary.}
The $\alpha$-coupling principle states that the soft aliveness scalar
produced by cumulative-damage P(F) must modulate all kinematic operations
on a particle, not just shape-matching constraints. This principle
generalizes the binary-removal semantic of non-differentiable mesh-cutting
algorithms to the continuous P(F) regime while preserving end-to-end
gradient flow for system identification. Section~\ref{sec:ablation}
quantifies the importance of each component (predict-coupling, persistence)
via ablation.

\subsection{Design Trade-offs vs Constraint-Based Residual Mapping}
\label{sec:design_tradeoff}

Our pipeline departs from Liang~\cite{liang2023} in PBD substrate choice:
Liang uses XPBD with distance/volume constraints~\cite{macklin2016}, while
we use M\"uller shape-matching~\cite{muller2005}. This choice has three
structural consequences that we acknowledge honestly to avoid overclaiming.

\paragraph{No per-constraint stiffness layer.}
In constraint-based PBD, per-particle stiffness $k$ is aggregated to
per-constraint stiffness via averaging,
$k_{c,i} = (1/|Q_i|)\sum_{q \in Q_i} k^q$, which is then consumed by the
projection step. Our shape-matching substrate skips this layer: per-particle
$k_i$ is applied directly in the apply step,
$\text{correction}_i = k_i \cdot (1 - 0.95 \cdot d_i) \cdot \delta_i$,
where $\delta_i$ is the cluster-averaged shape-matching delta.
This shortens the gradient chain by one Jacobian but means our $k_i$
does not have the per-constraint physical interpretation that $k_c$
has in Liang's formulation.

\paragraph{Physical interpretation of $k_i$.}
The cluster shape-matching goal positions
$g_i = R(q_i - \bar{q}) + \bar{c}$ depend on the alive-weighted centroid
$\bar{c}$ and the polar-decomposed rotation $R$ from $A_{pq}$. Neither
$R$ nor $\bar{c}$ depend on $k_i$: only the alive scalar $\alpha$
weights the centroid and covariance computations. Consequently $k_i$
functions as a per-particle \emph{relaxation rate} (controlling how much
of the cluster-suggested correction each particle accepts), rather than
as material Young's modulus. Recovered $k_i$ values must be interpreted
as ``per-particle PBD relaxation rates that, when used in our forward
simulator, reproduce the observed deformation trajectory'' rather than
as direct estimates of tissue mechanical stiffness. Calibration of $k_i$
scale to real material parameters requires a separate forward-only
identifiability study against known specimens (out of scope here).

\paragraph{Inner loop without geometric energy.}
Liang's inner residual mapping uses both Chamfer distance $D$ and a
constraint energy $E(x_t + \Delta_t) = \tfrac{1}{2} C^\top \mathrm{diag}(k'_c) C$
(their Eq.~5), where $k'_c$ is a uniform user-defined constant. The
energy term provides geometric prior for sub-surface particles that
have no direct observation (under occluded RGB-D capture). Crucially,
$k'_c$ must be decoupled from the outer-loop variable $k$ to avoid
a degenerate feedback where shrinking $k$ also shrinks $k'_c$, weakening
the inner constraint and producing a self-confirming low residual.

Our inner loop omits the geometric energy term ($\lambda_E = 0$ in
production), retaining only the observation-alignment terms
$L_\text{pos} + L_\text{cav}$. This avoids Liang's degenerate feedback
structurally (since $k_i$ appears in neither inner loss term through a
path that creates fake gradient), but at the cost of geometric prior
for sub-surface inference. Sub-surface particle behavior in our pipeline
is constrained implicitly by cluster shape-matching's SVD-based rotation
$R$, which provides structural rigidity coupling even without an explicit
energy term.

This design is appropriate for dense-observation synthetic setups
(Section~\ref{sec:results}) but is a known limitation for high-occlusion
phantom data. Future work re-enables $L_E$ with a $k'_c$-analog constant
(detached from outer $k$ via the \texttt{.item()} pattern used elsewhere
in our pipeline) when sub-surface occlusion exceeds the threshold at
which cluster-based rigidity alone provides sufficient internal constraint.

\paragraph{Per-particle $\tau$ field (Stage E).}
Our M\"uller-based substrate enables an extension Liang does not have:
the threshold parameter $\tau$ (fracture toughness) becomes per-particle
in the soft removal kernel, optimized jointly with per-particle stiffness.
This yields two co-recovered material fields $(k, \tau) \in
\mathbb{R}^N \times \mathbb{R}^N$ rather than just one, modelling
heterogeneous tissue where tumor regions differ from background in both
stiffness and brittleness. The cost is a doubled outer optimization
dimension, partially mitigated by independent sigmoid bounding on each
field.

\paragraph{Summary of trade-offs.}
Our design is appropriate when (a) observations are dense (per-particle
position rather than surface-only point cloud), (b) recovery of
spatial heterogeneity in both stiffness and toughness is desired, and
(c) the computational benefit of skipping per-constraint aggregation
is meaningful. Liang's constraint-based design is more appropriate when
sub-surface inference under occlusion is critical and only one
material field needs recovery. We do not claim a strict improvement;
the two designs target overlapping but distinct application regimes.
