\subsection{System Identification Framework}
\label{sec:sysid-framework}

% --- Paragraph 1: Loss design for sys-ID ---
System identification treats Algorithm~\ref{alg:ssa-jacobi} as a
differentiable forward map
$\theta \mapsto \mathbf{y}_{\text{sim}}(\theta)$ and uses Adam to fit
measured signals $\mathbf{y}_{\text{real}}$. To avoid the long-horizon
gradient bias that accumulates across many PBD
substeps~\cite{antonova2022, suh2022}, each Adam step runs a single
fresh forward substep from the rest configuration, and
cutting-parameter losses compare only the final cavity geometry. The
per-step loss is force-displacement MSE for elastic stiffness (in the
spirit of Kolawole's volume-MSE inverse FEM~\cite{kolawole2023}, with
displacement as the boundary input and force as the target), and a
soft-IoU plus signed-distance match on the continuous $\alpha$ field
(\ref{eq:soft-removal}) for cutting parameters.

% --- Paragraph 2: 3-vs-2 decision matrix ---
Three of our five physical parameters fall in this single-substep
regime: bulk stiffness $k$, damage threshold $\alpha_{\min}$, and
sharpness $s$. We verify in \S\ref{sec:sysid-synth} that Adam recovers
all three to within a few percent on synthetic ground truth in under a
minute. Friction and damping, by contrast, require non-zero particle
velocity to develop signal and are therefore not single-substep
identifiable; we adopt literature values ($\mu$ from
Misra~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{misra2010}, $\beta$ from
Mackle~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{mackle2020}), consistent with the
convention in DiSECt~\cite{heiden2021disect} and the cardiac inverse
FEM work of Kolawole~\cite{kolawole2023}.
